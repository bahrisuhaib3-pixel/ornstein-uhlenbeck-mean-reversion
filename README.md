This project develops a walk-forward mean reversion backtest built around the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck framework, which is commonly used to model stationary price behavior. The strategy dynamically recalculates mean reversion strength using rolling historical data and only initiates trades when the surrounding market regime appears stable.

To reduce structural model risk, the system integrates a state-space Kalman filter to estimate short-term trend components. It also applies a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) metric to measure how much directional movement reflects persistent drift versus random fluctuation. Together, these filters are meant to keep the strategy out of environments where prices are driven by sustained trends instead of reverting toward equilibrium.

Mean reversion models rely on the assumption that prices fluctuate around a relatively stable equilibrium level. In strongly trending markets, that assumption no longer holds, as price movement is influenced more by persistent drift than by stationary variation. By explicitly screening out those regimes, the strategy aims to operate only when the statistical assumptions underlying the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process remain valid.
